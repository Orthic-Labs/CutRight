Claude Code · two skills

Turn a raw clip into a finished short.

Two Claude Code skills. The first one takes your rambly, redone-takes camera footage and cuts it into a tight little story. The second one makes it look good — zooms, lens effects, animated text. You bring the clip; the AI does the boring part.

01 — What you get

Two skills that run in order

cutaway — the rough cut. WhisperX forced alignment finds the exact start and end of every word, so the AI cuts on real word edges, removes only the silences (the gaps where you say nothing), and arranges your best takes into one story: hook → … → call to action. You get a cut list and a matching MP4.

finish — the styling pass. Reframe to vertical, animated zooms and punch-ins, lens effects, an exponential text fade-in, the "authority stack" text look, captions, and sound effects timed to the motion.

These skills teach the method, not a pile of finished assets. The cut is fully turnkey. The finish skill gives you the techniques — you recreate the look with your own footage, your own sound effects, and your own style graphics. That small library is yours to keep.

02 — Install

Drop them into your skills folder

Copy each folder into your Claude Code skills directory:

cp -R cutaway ~/.claude/skills/shortform-cutaway
cp -R finish  ~/.claude/skills/shortform-finish

Then hand Claude Code a clip and say what you want — "make the rough cut, remove the silences" for cutaway, or once the cut is locked, "finish this short, add the hook zoom" for finish. The skills trigger themselves.

One-time setup for cutaway

The cut needs WhisperX, in its own Python 3.11 virtual environment. One time only:

python3.11 -m venv ~/wx-env
~/wx-env/bin/pip install whisperx

That's the only dependency. The cut scripts take every path as an argument, so there is nothing to hardcode — point the skill at your venv and your clip and go.

03 — First run

Which editor do you use?

On the first run each skill asks which editor you edit in and follows that branch. The cut math is identical for everyone — the only difference is how the cut lands in your tool.

a

DaVinci Resolve

The cut becomes a timeline. Zooms are built as Fusion comps with animation injected as splines.

b

Premiere Pro

The cut becomes an EDL/XML you import as a sequence. Zooms and text via keyframes + Essential Graphics.

c

Remotion

Everything is code. The cut is a playlist of segments; effects are interpolate curves.

d

Claude-Code-only

No NLE. The cut renders straight to an MP4 with ffmpeg. Nothing else to install.

No editor? Pick Claude-Code-only. It produces a finished, postable MP4 on its own.
04 — Cutaway

The rough cut, step by step

  • Extract the audio — ffmpeg pulls a mono 16k track from your clip.
  • Align the words — WhisperX force-aligns every word to the waveform, giving exact start/end times. This is the whole trick: real word edges, not guesses.
  • Pick the red thread — the AI reads the words and writes a beats list: one clean take per beat, dropping false starts, doubles, ums, and meta chatter, so the story moves forward.
  • Build the cut — clips snap to whole words; only the no-word gaps are removed. A single dial (GAP) controls punchy vs breathy.
  • Land it in your editor — timeline, EDL, Remotion segments, or a finished MP4.

05 — Finish

The styling pass

Work on a duplicate of the locked cut, then layer in:

  • Zooms — a zoom-out hook to open, punch-in/out waves to hide mid-video cuts inside constant motion. Front-loaded ease-out curves; motion blur gated to the move only.
  • Exponential text fade-in — text blooms in fast then settles slow, with a small upward drift and blur-to-sharp. Never a flat linear fade.
  • The authority-stack text look — words rise out of a soft fog and speed-ramp to a settle: bold, tight, deliberate. The brand-signature text move.
  • Sound that matches the motion — whoosh for a zoom, click for a snap, tick for a toggle, each peak-aligned to the exact frame. Plus the reverb-throw move at cut points.

06 — Make it yours

Building your own motion graphics

Here's the honest part again: the finish skill teaches you the moves, but the actual graphics — the little animations, the icons that fly in, the text that snaps — those are yours to build. Good news, you're not starting from a blank canvas. This is exactly how I do it.

Jitter — for inspiration and for building

jitter.video is basically Figma for motion. It runs in your browser, no After Effects, no learning curve from hell. Use it two ways. One, for inspiration — scroll what people are making, and when a move makes you go "oh, that's clean," screenshot it and rebuild it yourself. Two, for building — make your own branded animations fast and export them.

Lottie files — the cheat code

Lottie is a tiny animation format (under the hood it's just JSON). Jitter exports straight to it, and there are thousands of free ones on lottiefiles.com. So the move is simple: find one that's close to what you want, download it, crack it open, and mess with it — recolor it, change the timing, swap the text — until it's yours. Reverse-engineering someone else's animation is honestly the fastest way to learn the moves and stack up a little library that looks like you.

Getting it into your edit

  • Remotion — drops in straight. It renders Lottie natively (@remotion/lottie); hand it the JSON and it just plays.
  • Resolve / Premiere — render the Lottie out as a transparent video (ProRes 4444, or WebM with alpha), then drop it on a track above your footage.
You're not designing from scratch — you're collecting moves you like, making them yours, and reusing them. Do that a handful of times and you suddenly have a style.
07 — FAQ

Questions

Do I need DaVinci Resolve?

No. Resolve is one of four branches. Remotion, Premiere, or the Claude-Code-only ffmpeg path all work — and the ffmpeg path needs no editor at all, producing a finished MP4 by itself.

What footage does this work on?

Any 9:16 talking-head clip — you talking to camera, redoing takes, pausing, pointing. The messier the raw take, the more the cutaway earns its keep.

Why WhisperX and not plain Whisper?

Plain Whisper only guesses word times and drifts badly around pauses — it'll place a word inside a two-second silence. WhisperX force-aligns each word to the audio with a phoneme model, giving exact edges. That accuracy is the difference between "close" and clean.

Do I get the visual style assets?

No — and that's deliberate. The cut is turnkey, but the finished look is built from your own footage, sound effects, and graphics. The finish skill teaches you exactly how to make each move so the style is genuinely yours.

Is it fast?

WhisperX adds about one to two minutes per clip on a CPU (much faster on an NVIDIA GPU). After that the cut is near-instant. Most of your time goes into the styling pass, by choice.
